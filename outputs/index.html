Truth in labeling

Read this file as a file.

The essay below is a public-health data essay, not a clinical tool. It stays inside the boundaries of the attached CSV and says so whenever a claim reaches the edge.

  • 768 Pima Indian women aged 21+
  • Data collected in the 1980s
  • No HbA1c or lipid measurements
  • 8 features + 1 outcome label
  • 500 non-diabetic, 268 diabetic
  • Zero values are treated as possible missing records, not measured lows
  • No conclusion generalizes to other populations
  • Predictions are model outputs, not diagnoses

02 / The anchor

All 768 women on one glucose-risk scatter

Glucose is the strongest single signal in this file. Every dot is one woman; the vertical position is her out-of-fold logistic predicted probability, and the color is her actual outcome label. Threshold pins turn probabilities into decisions.

STEP 1

Read the field

Glucose spans 0 to 199 mg/dL. The small dots are real women; the diagonal band is the model's rising risk as glucose climbs.

STEP 2

At the 0.5 pin

Out-of-fold logistic predictions, threshold 0.5: false positives sit above the line in teal, false negatives below it in red.

STEP 3

A threshold is a choice

Raise or lower the pin and the trade between false positives and false negatives moves. No single number is the truth.

Five-model benchmark scorecard
